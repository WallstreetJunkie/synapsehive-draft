Delivery is the product

What you are actually choosing between.

When delivery is under pressure, there are usually three ways to add capacity. Each solves a different problem.

Option one

CV-led staffing

You get candidates. Your team evaluates technical fit, runs the interviews and owns the onboarding.

  • Technical assessment stays on your side
  • You compare candidates individually
  • Best fit when you want to run selection and onboarding in-house

Option two

A fixed-scope project house

You define the scope upfront and hand over responsibility for the project.

  • Changes usually mean changing the agreement
  • The external team owns delivery outside your day-to-day engineering structure
  • Best fit when the outcome is defined and you want someone else to own the scope

Fit

When this works, and when it doesn’t.

This tends to work well when

  • You have just raised, and the roadmap is now bigger than the team that wrote it.
  • You are behind on a release and hiring will not close the gap in time.
  • You have a platform or AI initiative that the current team cannot staff without stopping something else.
  • You are building a new engineering hub and need a senior core before hiring around it.
  • You need one senior specialist for a clearly defined role and want technical review before the introduction.

This is the wrong answer when

  • The decision will be made mainly on hourly rate. We are not the cheapest option and we do not bid to be.
  • You want a sealed scope built outside your organisation and handed back at the end. A project house is a better fit.
  • Your process requires a volume shortlist and multiple competitive interview rounds. We introduce teams or named specialists after technical review, not a candidate funnel.
  • Nobody on your side can stay involved in the engagement. Embedded teams need a counterpart.
